Executive Abstract

In mountainous, cloud-obscured disaster environments such as the Himalayan river basins of Nepal, rapid post-disaster flood response is severely constrained by monsoonal cloud cover, steep-terrain radar shadowing, and extreme topography. This project implements a fully verified, end-to-end multimodal geospatial intelligence platform for Track B: Mapping Flood Damage from Space. Combining multi-temporal Sentinel-1 Radiometric Terrain Corrected (RTC) synthetic aperture radar (SAR), high-resolution Copernicus DEM (30m), deep Siamese neural segmentation (SNUNet-ECAM), independent physical backscatter thresholding, and historical OpenStreetMap (OSM) topological routing, the system directly answers the three core disaster rescue questions without fabricating observations or leaking post-disaster labels. Validated against Copernicus EMS Rapid Mapping activation EMSR927, the system demonstrates high classical backscatter sensitivity to GLOF debris flows (37.6 ha overlap) alongside strict neural water discrimination.

1. Problem Definition & Operational Architecture

1.1 The Operational Rescue Questions

During extreme hydrological surges in steep river valleys, emergency disaster coordinators face three critical operational bottlenecks:

  1. Where has water inundated land? Determining active flood extent despite heavy monsoon cloud cover that completely blinds conventional optical satellites.
  2. Which transport infrastructure is submerged or severed? Rapidly identifying which primary and secondary highway corridors intersect flood water to prevent civilian convoys from entering submerged road sections.
  3. Which human settlements are isolated? Modeling road network reachability to calculate which mountainous communities have lost vehicular access to regional healthcare and emergency facilities.

1.2 End-to-End System Architecture

The platform operates as a decoupled, modular pipeline integrating spaceborne data acquisition, neural inference, and real-time interactive visualization:

  • Satellite Acquisition Service: Communicates anonymously with Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC API to discover and SAS-sign Sentinel-1 RTC products and Copernicus DEM tiles matching exact spatial and temporal bounds.
  • Tiled Deep Inference Engine: Re-projects radar backscatter and DEM elevation to a common 10m UTM Zone 45N grid, normalizing inputs and executing forward inference through SNUNet-ECAM using 2D Hann window blending across overlapping 512×512 patches.
  • Independent Physical Baseline: Simultaneously computes calibrated decibel difference (ΔVV < -3 dB) and 2nd-order slope filtering (< 15°) to provide an unlearned physical reference.
  • Historical OSM Infrastructure Pipeline: Extracts pre-disaster highways, bridges, settlements, and hospitals from the OpenStreetMap Historical Attic Database with a strict cutoff timestamp of 2026-07-27T00:00:00Z.
  • Topological Network Graph Router: Builds an edge-weighted NetworkX graph from OSM highways, removes flood-severed edges, and evaluates settlement reachability via Dijkstra shortest-path algorithms.
  • Tile Streaming & Point Inspector: Exposes dynamic Web Mercator (EPSG:3857) raster tiles and coordinate-level physical radar sampling with deterministic scientific explanations.

2. Data Pipeline & Geospatial Methodology

2.1 Sentinel-1 SAR RTC Acquisition & Orbit Matching

A fundamental failure mode in satellite radar change detection is comparing scenes acquired from different viewing geometries, which introduces massive false-positive backscatter variation due to terrain aspect and radar foreshortening. The platform enforces strict orbit geometry filtering:

  • Identical Orbit Constraints: The pipeline strictly queries Sentinel-1 scenes from Relative Orbit 19 in the Descending pass direction.
  • Observation Baseline: Pre-event scene S1A_IW_GRDH_1SDV_20260824T001857... (24 August 2026) and post-event scene S1A_IW_GRDH_1SDV_20260905T001857... (5 September 2026). This 12-day repeat pass represents the earliest cloud-penetrating post-disaster observation over the Trishuli corridor.
  • Radiometric Terrain Correction: The pipeline uses RTC gamma-naught (γ°) backscatter in both VV and VH polarizations, pre-corrected for local incidence angle and terrain scattering area.

2.2 Digital Elevation Model (DEM) & Slope Physics

In Himalayan environments, steep mountainsides reflect radar signals away from the sensor, producing low backscatter resembling water (radar geometric shadow). Furthermore, flood water cannot physically accumulate on steep slopes:

  • Copernicus DEM (GLO-30): 30m native elevation data is reprojected and bilinearly resampled to match the 10m SAR analysis grid.
  • 2nd-Order Central Difference Slope: Terrain slope is derived in degrees using physical metric coordinates ($dx = dy = 10$ m): $$\text{Slope} = \arctan\left(\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right)^2}\right) \cdot \frac{180}{\pi}$$
  • Slope Filter Mask: Any radar drop occurring on slopes $\ge 15.0^\circ$ is excluded from candidate water masks, eliminating false positives on mountain ridges.

2.3 Unpadded AOI Footprint vs. Padded Processing Grid

To eliminate spatial convolution boundary artifacts during neural inference and central difference gradient calculation, a 200m peripheral buffer is applied to create a $1,140 \times 1,039$ pixel processing grid. However, all headline area statistics and vector outputs are clipped strictly to the unpadded requested AOI:

2.4 Unlearned Classical Change Baseline

In parallel with deep neural inference, an independent numerical physics baseline converts linear radar power to decibels ($VV_{dB} = 10 \cdot \log_{10}(VV)$) and calculates backscatter difference: $$\Delta VV_{dB} = VV_{post, dB} - VV_{pre, dB}$$ Pixels exhibiting $\Delta VV_{dB} < -3.0$ dB on terrain with $\text{Slope} < 15.0^\circ$ form the classical candidate change mask (138.1 ha / 1.3810 km²), providing an unlearned physical reference.

3. AI Modeling, Provenance & EMSR927 Validation

3.1 SNUNet-ECAM Siamese Neural Network

The primary flood segmentation model is SNUNet-ECAM (Siamese U-Net++ with Ensemble Channel Attention Module), a state-of-the-art multi-temporal architecture designed for bi-temporal remote-sensing change detection:

  • Siamese Backbone: Processes pre-event and post-event multi-channel tensors ($VV, VH, DEM$) through shared convolutional encoders, extracting multi-scale hierarchical feature representations.
  • Dense Skip Connections & ECAM: Channel attention dynamically suppresses noise and emphasizes informative spectral channels across temporal difference features.
  • Inference Normalization: Checkpoint weights (trained on the global Kuro Siwo dataset) expect standardized inputs. Tensors are normalized using global Kuro Siwo statistics ($VV_\mu=0.0818, VV_\sigma=0.1772, VH_\mu=0.0163, VH_\sigma=0.0489, DEM_\mu=175.75, DEM_\sigma=296.88$).
  • Tiled Hann Window Stitching: Overlapping 512×512 inference patches are weighted by a 2D Hann window, eliminating tile boundary seams and edge distortion.

3.2 Model Provenance & Checkpoint Integrity

HONEST PROVENANCE & WEIGHT VERIFICATION: The system utilizes the official pretrained Kuro Siwo SNUNet-ECAM checkpoint (Bountos et al., NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks Track; checkpoints/best_segmentation.pt, 144,657,509 bytes ~138 MB, SHA-256: 6c6bcb78d956f9d139743eb5bc44cf8e14528b643f9cbf5f4478f4e321a609ed). Due to strict hackathon compute and time constraints, local fine-tuning was not conducted; inference relies on the pretrained global model using Kuro Siwo channel normalization.

3.3 Independent Reference Validation against Copernicus EMS EMSR927

To evaluate model and change detection behavior against official emergency response intelligence, outputs were benchmarked against Copernicus Emergency Management Service Rapid Mapping activation EMSR927 (GDACS: FL1104124), component EMSR927_AOI03_GRA_PRODUCT_v1 (Bidur AOI), delineated from 0.8m–1.0m VHR optical imagery (BlackSky and Satellogic, 27 August 2026). Area Product Reconciliations: Across the full unpadded AOI (109.51 km²), SNUNet predicts 15.76 ha of raw Class 2 flood raster, filtering to 2.87 ha of vectorised flood extent (41 polygons after the 500 m² minimum-area filter). Crucial Evaluation Caveat: EMSR927 is an optical mass-movement reference used exclusively for cross-hazard spatial-overlap analysis (landslide/debris co-occurrence). It is not direct standing-water ground truth. Its precision (24.19%–24.58%), recall (0.10%–0.28%), and IoU (0.09%–0.28%) evaluate spatial co-occurrence with event deposits and must never be presented as validated flood-water segmentation accuracy.

4. Infrastructure Exposure & Topological Connectivity

4.1 Historical OSM Extraction with Strict Temporal Cutoff

To prevent post-event crowd-sourced mapping bias from contaminating the pre-disaster baseline, all infrastructure was queried from the OpenStreetMap Historical Attic Database with a strict cutoff of 2026-07-27T00:00:00Z (one month prior to the event). Verified via the HeiGIT ohsome API, 898 routable highway ways, 25 bridges, 27 settlements, and 5 healthcare facilities were cataloged. A 2.5 km peripheral road network buffer was applied to prevent false border disconnections.

4.2 Conservative Corridor Closure vs. Fragment-Level Sensitivity

RECONCILED ROUTING METRICS & CLOSURE ASSUMPTIONS:
• Direct Submerged Road Length: 190.1 meters — Aggregate length of highway geometry directly intersecting modelled flood polygons across 12 unique OSM highway ways.
• Baseline Scenario (Conservative Corridor Closure): Assumes entire OSM ways are closed when any section intersects flood water. Removes 1,436 graph edges (26.14 km) (including ~25.95 km outside the mapped flood intersection) to model operational corridor closure and structural unreliability. Under this scenario: 2 settlements potentially cut off (Laharepauwa, Dhunge), 2 detoured (Tupche [+0.68 km], Uttargaya [+4.42 km]), and 23 unaffected (including Betrawati).
• Alternative Scenario (Localized Fragment Sensitivity): Removes strictly the 25 graph edge fragments (383.9 m) physically intersecting flood polygons. Under this scenario: 0 cut off, 2 detoured (Tupche, Uttargaya), and 25 unaffected.
• Crucial Caveat: The 2 cut-off settlements reflect the conservative corridor closure assumption, not confirmed real-world isolation. 26.14 km is not submerged; 190.1 m is the aggregate submerged road length.

4.3 Metric Road Network Graph Construction

The routing pipeline constructs a formal weighted graph $G = (V, E)$ using NetworkX:

  • Nodes ($V$): 28,323 metric coordinates rounded to 1m precision in UTM Zone 45N.
  • Edges ($E$): 28,512 line segments weighted by their exact Euclidean length in meters. Non-routable tags (footways, tracks under construction) are pruned.
  • Destination Snapping: 5 essential healthcare and administrative facilities are snapped to the nearest road network node (max distance 1,500m).

6. Limitations, Reproducibility & Mandatory Compliance

6.1 Known Operational Limitations

  • SAR Revisit Latency: While Sentinel-1 provides all-weather imaging through monsoon clouds, the 12-day orbital repeat cycle over ascending/descending swaths means the earliest post-event observation was 5 September 2026 (10 days after the event). Flash floods peak and recede rapidly; thus, water extents represent persistent inundation and high groundwater saturation rather than the instant of peak surge.
  • Building Inventory Completeness: Pre-disaster building extraction was query-capped at 1,000 features due to attic Overpass API gateway limits. Zero exposed buildings applies strictly to the mapped sample and does not verify complete structural safety across unmapped structures.
  • Mountain Shadow & Foreshortening: In ultra-steep Himalayan topography, radar shadowing blinds north/west-facing slopes. While DEM slope masking eliminates false positives, undetected water in radar shadows remains a physical limitation of side-looking radar.
